How I developed this

Why only draw the ship?

The plan says stage 1 is "draw the ship". I was tempted to make it move as well, but drawing it first meant I could get its size and shape right without anything moving. It's a small step, and it's easy to see whether it worked.

Decision: x and y are the centre

I could have made (x, y) the top-left corner, as rect() does by default. I chose the centre because:

  • starting in the middle of the screen is just width / 2;
  • bullets will fire from the centre of the ship, so its centre will be needed anyway;
  • it matches the balls in sketches 06–13, which were positioned by their centre.

This is a small decision, but every later stage depends on it, so I wrote it as a comment at the top of the class.

Decision: the cannon's size comes from the body's size

My first version had the cannon's size as fixed numbers. When I changed SHIP_WIDTH to try a bigger ship, the cannon stayed tiny. Working the cannon's size out from bodyWidth and bodyHeight means the whole ship scales together.

Why tests already?

There's hardly anything to test yet, so why write tests.js now? Because adding the testing setup is easiest while the program is small. From now on, each stage just adds one or two test functions to a file that already works.

I made each test fail once on purpose before trusting it. For example, I changed the expected x from 100 to 101, saw the red FAIL, then changed it back.

Manual checks

Some things are easier to check by eye than with a test. Before committing, I checked:

  • The ship is near the bottom, centred left to right.
  • The cannon sits on top of the body, centred, with no gap.
  • The console shows only PASS lines, and no errors.
