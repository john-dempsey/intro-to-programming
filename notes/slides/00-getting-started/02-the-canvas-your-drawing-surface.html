The canvas: your drawing surface

In p5, the canvas is the rectangular grid of pixels your sketch draws onto. Nothing appears on screen until a canvas exists, which is why every sketch in this module starts with a call to createCanvas():

createCanvas(640, 360);

The two arguments are the canvas's width and height, measured in pixels — this call creates a drawing surface 640 pixels wide and 360 pixels tall. p5 also stores these two numbers in two variables named width and height, which the rest of the sketch can read at any time.

The important thing to understand about the canvas is that it's a persistent bitmap: once a pixel is coloured, it stays that colour — visible on screen exactly as drawn — until something else draws over it. A canvas is not a description of shapes that p5 keeps redrawing from scratch; it's closer to a real canvas covered in paint. If nothing draws over a pixel, that pixel keeps whatever colour it was last given, forever. This single fact explains a lot of what looks strange the first time you see it in later sketches — for example, why an animated sketch has to repaint the whole canvas every frame to avoid old positions leaving a trail.
